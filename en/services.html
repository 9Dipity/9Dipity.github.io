    Pillar 2

    Custom Software Development

    Tools built for how your business actually works — not how generic software assumes it works.

    Off-the-shelf software is either too rigid, too expensive, or does 90% of what you need while missing the 10% that matters most. Custom software built on a proper database means your tools fit your business — and grow with it.

    Every project is scoped and priced individually

    Custom software can't have a menu price — the right solution depends entirely on what your business actually does, how many users you have, what systems already exist, and where the real complexity lies. A booking system for a one-person studio is a very different project from an inventory system syncing three locations and an online store.

    What I do instead: you describe your situation, I ask the right questions, and I come back with a clear scope and a fixed price before any work starts. No hourly billing, no open-ended engagements, no surprises at the end.

    What to expect: I'll reply within 24 hours with either a scoped proposal or a short call to gather what I need. Most projects are quoted within 2–3 business days of first contact.

    Tell me about your project

    What we typically build

    Every project is different, but these are the kinds of tools we build most often:

    • Inventory & stock management — unified across physical and online stores, with real-time sync
    • Booking & appointment systems — for clinics, service businesses, studios, and hospitality
    • Customer databases (CRM-lite) — purchase history, loyalty programmes, communication logs
    • Order management — from quote through fulfilment, with supplier tracking
    • Pricing & margin tools — supplier price import automation, margin calculators, competitive tracking
    • Internal reporting dashboards — live KPIs, sales by location/channel, staff performance
    • Process automation — eliminating repetitive manual work from your operations

    Technology & approach

    All custom software is built with C#/.NET, SQL Server, and a clean web front-end. I use dependency injection and proper test coverage — so the code is maintainable, testable, and not a black box you're afraid to touch. You get the source code. If you ever want to take it to another developer, you can.

    • C# / ASP.NET Core backend with REST APIs
    • SQL Server database with Dapper or EF Core
    • Modern HTML/CSS/JS front-end (no heavy frameworks)
    • Unit tests — I write them, not as an afterthought
    • Docker-ready, Azure DevOps CI/CD available

    How it's priced

    Full Package projects are scoped and quoted individually — there's no meaningful way to put a fixed price on a system before understanding what that system actually needs to do. The scope conversation is where the value happens: it's where we figure out what to build, in what order, and what you actually need vs. what sounds nice in theory.

    To give you a rough sense of scale: a straightforward project for a single-location business (one management tool + a public website + the database to connect them) typically comes in somewhere in the low thousands. A multi-location system with several integrated modules and e-commerce is a materially larger project. Both get a fixed price before any work starts.

    The process: describe your business and what you're trying to solve. I'll ask a few targeted questions, then come back with a clear scope document and a fixed price — usually within a week of first contact. No obligation to proceed.

    What a Full Package project looks like

    Here's an example of a typical project — a clothing boutique with a physical store and an online shop:

    • Database: Products, variants (size/colour), stock levels per location, suppliers, customers, orders
    • Management software: Web-based stock manager synced between store and WooCommerce, purchase order tracking, low-stock alerts, daily sales reports
    • Website: Public-facing product catalogue pulling live from the database, online store with Stripe checkout, multi-language (LV/EN)
    • Integration: When a sale happens online, stock updates in the management system automatically. When staff receive new stock, the website catalogue updates immediately.
    • Timeline: 6–10 weeks depending on scope
